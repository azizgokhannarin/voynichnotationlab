# H02 — Notation evolves during manuscript production

## Hypothesis

At least part of the Voynich notation repertoire may have developed or become motorically stabilized while the manuscript was being produced.

## Competing models

- **H02-A Stable:** the full notation inventory existed before manuscript production.
- **H02-B Evolving:** new signs/blocks were introduced or reshaped during production.
- **H02-C Multiple equivalent forms:** the same functional/sound unit could be written in several forms.
- **H02-D Apparent evolution only:** observed changes are caused by different scribal hands, section conventions, tools, or transcription choices.

## Predictions for an evolving system

For a genuinely later-introduced glyph, within the same scribal hand where possible:

- early examples may have greater morphological variance;
- later examples may become faster/simpler/more consistent;
- a control glyph already frequent at the beginning should show less change;
- innovation rate may be higher in an early production phase and later plateau.

## Critical confound

Bound folio order is not assumed to equal production chronology.
