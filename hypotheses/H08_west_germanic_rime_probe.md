# H08 — West Germanic rime-family probe

## Trigger

The high-frequency Voynich family

`aiin / daiin / saiin / kaiin / taiin`

has a surface resemblance to West Germanic

`ein / sein / mein / kein / dein`.

## Why it is worth testing

- the rime family is genuinely present in the corpus;
- the 8-like EVA `d` has independently been proposed as S/Ş-like;
- `daiin` would then be compatible with an S-initial member;
- fifteenth-century Germanic dialects provide historically plausible ein/mîn-dîn-sîn/kein
  families, with regional diphthongization.

## Why it is not accepted

- several required consonant mappings are unconstrained;
- Currier A/B rates differ strongly across candidate words;
- line-position behavior differs strongly;
- many additional `-aiin` prefixes have no immediate explanation;
- short phonetic strings are highly vulnerable to coincidence.

## Falsification target

A successful West Germanic model must use one fixed phoneme mapping and predict multiple
independent inflection/function-word families without remapping glyphs ad hoc.

## Status

Exploratory / low confidence.
