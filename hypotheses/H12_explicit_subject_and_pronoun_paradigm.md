# H12 — Short tokens may contain an explicit personal-pronoun paradigm

## Hypothesis

If the underlying language is relatively non-pro-drop, a small set of high-frequency short
Voynich tokens may encode personal pronouns or closely related subject markers.

## Distributional prediction

Different members should:

- share substantial successor/predicate distributions;
- occupy similar clause-level positions;
- form a small closed class;
- remain distinguishable from ordinary morphological variants.

## Gender sub-hypothesis

If the language marks third-person gender overtly, two or more members of the paradigm may
share syntactic distributions while diverging in semantic contexts.

This cannot be tested from illustrations alone.

## Falsification

The hypothesis weakens if no small short-token family shares predicate distributions, or if
most clause-like units systematically lack any overt short subject candidate.
