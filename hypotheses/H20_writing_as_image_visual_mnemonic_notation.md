# H20 — Writing as image: private visual mnemonic notation

## Hypothesis

The Voynich surface is a practiced, private and lossy visual mnemonic notation. It imitates the
appearance and motor rhythm of Western/Latin-script handwriting, while its units may recall mixed
levels of the writer's internal language: sounds, syllables, word shapes, abbreviations, phrases,
prosody or concepts.

The underlying language is unspecified. “Latin” in this hypothesis describes a possible script
tradition or learned manuscript aesthetic, not a demonstrated plaintext language.

## Mechanism

The writer uses a bounded inventory of motor/graphic chunks. A written form can be selected by:

- visual similarity to an internally remembered word or phrase;
- a partial sound or rhythmic cue;
- an illustration or topic cue;
- a familiar abbreviation-like gesture;
- local visual analogy to a recently written form;
- available line width and paragraph layout.

The notation may be many-to-one and one-to-many. Context and personal memory can supply information
that the surface does not preserve. Therefore no globally reversible key is required.

## Why it fits current results

- direct fixed-unit language recovery failed despite strong positive controls;
- phrase order did not transfer under four target languages;
- page/line allocation was strong while fixed-inventory within-line identity order was weak;
- line-locality could be reproduced by rendered and diplomatically abbreviated Latin;
- terminal choice contains strong graphic/layout effects;
- paragraph starts are graphically marked but no mandatory paragraph-final punctuation was found;
- zoomed-out pages preserve Western-handwriting texture without revealing stable Latin words;
- the mechanism is learnable and writable by a fifteenth-century human.

## Predictions

1. Normal-orientation Voynich crops should be judged more Western-manuscript-like than mirrored or
   stroke-order-destroyed controls, especially for initial/terminal rhythm.
2. Visual families should be recoverable more consistently from image strokes than from atomic EVA.
3. If the notation is meaningful, distant pages sharing independently coded illustration motifs
   should reuse visual families beyond local-copy, section, hand, page and line-position controls.
4. If the notation is merely structured pseudo-writing, visual similarity should be strongest to
   immediately preceding forms and should help fill remaining line width, without distant semantic
   recurrence.
5. Any external source-manuscript match should predict previously unexamined visual families or
   paragraph structure; resemblance discovered only after inspection is not evidence.
6. Human observers may agree on graphic families without agreeing on phonetic readings.

## Falsification and demotion

H20 is weakened if:

- a bounded, reproducible mapping recovers held-out natural-language phrase order;
- image-based visual families add no stability beyond EVA or page/hand effects;
- illustration-motif recurrence disappears under blinded distant-page controls;
- normal versus mirrored perception is indistinguishable for initial/terminal rhythm;
- the proposed visual mechanism requires token-specific exceptions or unlimited flexibility.

If only local copy/modify and line-fit predictions succeed, demote meaningful mnemonic notation
and prefer structured pseudo-writing. If distant motif-conditioned recurrence succeeds on held-out
folios, promote meaningful mnemonic notation without claiming a language or translation.

## Biographical interpretation boundary

It is plausible that the work began as private notation and later became more book-like because
the writer expected another reader to understand it. This narrative is not manuscript evidence.
No diagnosis, speech ability, neurotype, identity or motive is inferred from H20.
