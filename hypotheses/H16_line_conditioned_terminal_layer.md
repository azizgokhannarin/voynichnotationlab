# H16 — Part of the terminal system is a line-conditioned notation layer

## Hypothesis

At least some members of the provisional terminal inventory are selected partly by
manuscript layout rather than only by the underlying language.

## Strongest current candidate

EVA `m` is extraordinarily enriched at physical line endings even after conditioning on
the same normalized stem.

This makes `m` a high-priority notation/allography/closure candidate.

## Competing explanations

- line-conditioned allograph;
- abbreviation/closure convention;
- prosodic or phrase-final sign aligned with line breaks;
- true morphology whose syntax happens to correlate strongly with line ends.

## Falsification

The notation-layer interpretation weakens if the effect disappears after stronger
linguistic/clause-boundary controls or if equivalent layout effects are expected in matched
historical-language manuscripts.

## Status

Strong structural support for line conditioning; exact linguistic/graphic interpretation open.
