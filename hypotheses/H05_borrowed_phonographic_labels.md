# H05 — Borrowed technical names may be preserved phonographically

## Hypothesis

Some labels may preserve heard names for plants, substances, medicines, places, persons,
or imported objects, even if the manuscript's underlying language is different.

A possible source name may have travelled through multilingual medical/trade networks.

## Important distinction

A successful loanword match does not identify the manuscript language.

For example, a Turkic-, Arabic-, Persian-, Greek-, or Latin-derived technical name could
occur inside a manuscript written in another European language.

## First candidate test

The f78r label proposed elsewhere as "şarap otu" was tested without accepting the
translation in advance.

Result: the full semantic reading is currently too weak to use as an anchor, but its first
EVA `da` block remains a phonetic candidate for independent testing.
