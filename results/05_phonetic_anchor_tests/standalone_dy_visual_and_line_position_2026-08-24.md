# Visual confirmation and positional test of standalone EVA `dy`

Date: 2026-08-24

## Visual confirmation

A manually highlighted two-glyph token on f78r was independently matched to RF1b-EVA locus
`f78r.13`.

RF1b-EVA transcribes the end of that locus as:

    ... qotee@152;y.dy

Therefore the highlighted two-glyph token is the standalone EVA token:

    dy

The first glyph is the 8-like EVA `d`; the second is EVA `y`.

## Corpus frequency

Across the complete RF1b-EVA input:

- standalone `dy`: 205 tokens
- all occurrences of `dy` as a pair: 5009
- token-final `dy` within longer tokens: 4665
- standalone `dy`: 205
- medial `dy`: 77
- token-initial `dy` in longer tokens: 62

This confirms that `dy` is overwhelmingly associated with token termination.

## Is standalone `dy` merely a line-ending artifact?

Standalone `dy` by line position:

| Position | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| line medial | 131 | 63.90% |
| line final | 72 | 35.12% |
| line initial | 1 | 0.49% |
| only token on line | 1 | 0.49% |

For comparison:

- arbitrary token line-final baseline: ~13.98%
- all two-character tokens line-final baseline: ~17.02%
- standalone `dy` line-final: ~35.12%

Thus standalone `dy` is strongly enriched at line ends.

However, **most standalone `dy` tokens (63.9%) are still line-medial**. It cannot be explained
only as a line-end filler.

A further useful control:

- all tokens ending in `dy`: 4870
- among them line-final: 639 = 13.12%

This is approximately the ordinary token line-final baseline.

Therefore the line-end enrichment is specific to the **standalone token `dy`**, not to the
suffix `-dy` in general.

## Interpretation

This sharpens the competing models.

### Model P — phonographic short word / syllable

`dy` is a two-symbol phonographic word or syllabic item. Its line-final enrichment could
reflect syntax/discourse position.

### Model M — morphological ending

Within longer tokens, `-dy` behaves as a highly productive terminal construction. The
standalone form could be a related particle/clitic.

### Model O — structural operator

`dy` could be a boundary/closure operator rather than ordinary phonetic content.

### Model L — line-management effect

The standalone form may participate partly in scribal line closure, but this cannot explain
the majority of medial occurrences and does not explain the general token-final `-dy`
distribution.

## Important consequence

The user's visual `8g` observation is confirmed as the same EVA `dy` block discovered
statistically.

This is stronger than an isolated visual resemblance because the block has a reproducible,
highly asymmetric corpus distribution.

It still does **not** establish a phonetic value for EVA `d` or EVA `y`.

## Next test

Compare the local contexts around:

1. standalone `dy`;
2. tokens ending in `-dy`;
3. matched tokens ending in other common terminal blocks.

If standalone `dy` shares predecessor/successor distributions with a grammatical function
word or clitic, that favors a linguistic interpretation. If it is mainly predicted by line
geometry, that favors a scribal/structural interpretation.
