# Paragraph and marginal-item scan — exploratory report v1

## Decision

The manuscript has a strong, independently replicated paragraph-initial graphic layer. Direct
EVA gallows (`k/t/p/f`) begin 615/740 ZL3b transcriber-defined paragraphs (83.11%), compared with
293/3,390 other running lines (8.64%; corrected odds ratio 51.76). IT2a independently gives
627/772 (81.22%) versus 285/3,346 (8.52%; odds ratio 46.25).

This is evidence for a paragraph-initial function. It does not yet decide whether the tall form is
a capital-like allograph, an opening marker, a formula-initial sound unit, or a combination of
these roles.

## Findings

| Test | ZL3b | IT2a | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Direct gallows at paragraph start | 83.11% | 81.22% | Strong paragraph-initial association |
| Direct gallows at other line start | 8.64% | 8.52% | Not merely a general line-initial habit |
| Bench/composite gallows inside first token at paragraph start | 2.03% | 2.98% | No strong paragraph-specific enrichment |
| Bench/composite gallows inside first token at other line start | 2.01% | 2.06% | Composite construction also behaves as an ordinary internal unit |
| Terminal `m` at paragraph end | 9.19% | 9.97% | Not a paragraph-final punctuation candidate |
| Terminal `m` at other physical line end | 16.73% | 17.90% | Better explained as physical-line-final realization |

The user's highlighted nested construction therefore remains important, but for a different
reason: it supports a compositional writing operation in which a tall outer frame can contain
ordinary-sized internal marks. Its present distribution does not make it a paragraph-only
capital. The outer frame, interior sequence, pedestal/bench and ornamental extension must be
recorded separately before any phonetic value is proposed.

Among common final EVA characters, `n` is moderately enriched at paragraph ends in both
transcriptions (ZL3b odds ratio 1.66; IT2a 1.72), and `y` is more weakly enriched (1.37 and 1.33).
These are candidates for image-grounded follow-up, not identified punctuation marks.

## Boundary reliability

ZL3b and IT2a agree on paragraph loci with Jaccard 0.9188; ZL3b and GC2a with 0.9299. This high
agreement shows that much of the layout signal is visually conspicuous. It is not ground truth:
the transcribers made segmentation decisions, and their disagreement is concentrated in difficult
pages such as the final starred section.

ZL3b paragraphs have a median length of 4 physical lines and 39 tokens. The range is 2–32 lines,
showing that a fixed line-count template is not a sufficient account.

## Stars are not identical to visual paragraphs

The 23 Quire-20 recipe pages contain 324 reported marginal stars. The same pages contain 285 ZL3b,
289 IT2a and 321 GC2a paragraph starts. GC2a has effectively treated most stars as boundaries;
ZL3b and IT2a have preserved much larger blocks on several pages.

This does not refute the interpretation of a star as an item marker. It makes a sharper claim
necessary: a star may mark an item or subentry inside a visually continuous paragraph. On f108v,
for example, the source reports 16 stars but probably only 8 visual paragraphs, with the last seven
stars beside one long paragraph. On f111r it reports 17 stars but about 6 paragraphs; on f111v,
19 stars but about 8. On f116r, multiple stars occur beside a long paragraph and the final two
paragraphs have none.

Therefore the next image annotation must preserve two independent layers:

1. geometric paragraph boundary;
2. marginal star/item boundary.

Collapsing them would manufacture the conclusion before the test.

## Punctuation consequence

No punctuation glyph has been identified. The useful result is narrower:

- opening structure is strongly marked by tall/ornate forms;
- EVA `m` is a physical-line ending, not a paragraph stop;
- `n` and `y` merit paragraph-final image checks;
- marginal stars appear to encode an item layer that only partly overlaps paragraph geometry.

The proposed idea that punctuation may be expressed by a letter-like opening convention remains
testable. It must be tested through position and graphic transformation, not through an assumed
Latin name such as *period* or an assigned sound `p`.

## Authorization and stop rule

This report is exploratory because its paragraph labels originate in transcriptions. The next
authorized step is a blind image-geometry annotation in which first glyphs and marginal stars are
hidden during the primary boundary decision. If the image-only boundary calls do not reproduce the
gallows enrichment, the paragraph-initial claim is withdrawn. No language mapping or phonetic
assignment is authorized by this result.
