# Final claim ledger — v6.0.0

## Evidence-backed claims

1. The surface is graphically and spatially organised, not independent symbol randomness.
2. The tested fixed-unit/fixed-boundary bounded language mappings did not recover held-out phrase
   order across German, Italian, French or Latin, despite strong positive controls.
3. No tested surface statistic identifies broad hidden language, structured records or autonomous
   generation; these broad classes were underidentified in the previous representation.
4. Paragraph starts have strong direct-gallows enrichment across two transcriptions.
5. No EVA terminal or bounded preceding-ending-conditioned terminal mapping behaves as a mandatory
   paragraph-final point on TRAIN; the instrument detects an injected rule nearly perfectly.
6. Zoomed-out pages preserve Western-handwriting texture; this does not identify a language.

## Leading interpretation

H20, private lossy visual mnemonic notation or “writing as image,” is the leading working
hypothesis because it jointly accommodates the negative language/cipher results, graphic fluency,
layout sensitivity, paragraph design and perceptual text-likeness with a fifteenth-century
human-executable mechanism.

## Still competing

Structured pseudo-writing generated through local visual/motor analogy remains open. H20 is not
promoted to meaningful notation until distant illustration-motif-conditioned visual recurrence or
an external source challenge succeeds on held-out material.

## Prohibited claims

- The manuscript has been deciphered.
- H20 is proven.
- The underlying language is Latin or any named language.
- The marks are independent randomness.
- The writer had a particular diagnosis, neurotype, speech ability, identity or motive.
- Failure of tested language models proves that no mental content existed.
- Visual resemblance alone supplies a phonetic reading.

## Program rule

No further unrestricted language brute force, generator enrichment or post-hoc surface metric is
authorised. Only preregistered image-grounded tests distinguishing meaningful mnemonic notation
from structured pseudo-writing may continue.
